{{CONTEXT}}
What past seasons show
  • Melt starts at the grounding zone and in the blue-ice belt, then spreads along the channels to the terminal lake; the ice front comes last (Dell et al. 2024; project analysis).
  • Zone A, 2002–25: first wet day median 11 Dec, earliest 8 Nov (2007), latest 22 Dec. Recent: 21 Nov 2017, 5 Dec 2019, 10 Dec 2024, 27 Nov 2025.
  • Channel water builds from about 12–20 Dec; in lake years water reaches the terminal lake 27 Dec–11 Jan.
  • Snowfall delays ponding; the five warmest seasons by degree days were the five biggest melt seasons.
How this page works
  • Wet snow: AMSR2 microwave product (Picard & Fily, Grenoble), 10 km, daily, about 10 days behind.
  • Sentinel-1 radar: every few days through cloud; change from the October median of the same orbit. Wet snow is darker; 3 dB is the usual mark.
  • Optical (Sentinel-2, Landsat): clear days only; open water must also be at least twice as dark as in October, which keeps blue ice out. Below about 2 km² in zone A, low sun can give false water.
  • NISAR: L-band radar every 12 days, also senses water below the surface; provisional data.
  • Stations: Ogimet reports every 6 h and NOAA daily values. Forecast: AMPS, from the MINIS weather site.
  • Checked daily, station reports every 6 h, until the end of February 2027. Code and rules: github.com/vikramonice/nivl-melt-watch.
